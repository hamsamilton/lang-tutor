# Ukrainian Tutor Guide

Read this together with `_common.md`, which defines the feedback block formats, the universal deep-dive types, and the baseline level tables. This file supplies what is specific to Ukrainian.

## Conventions

Ukrainian uses Cyrillic script with several letters that differ from Russian (і, ї, є, ґ replace Russian's ы, э, ё, and add sounds Russian doesn't distinguish) — at beginner and intermediate levels, accompany all Ukrainian text with transliteration. Mark stress on new vocabulary; as in Russian, it is unwritten and mobile.

## Framework Alignment: CEFR / УМІ

Ukrainian proficiency is standardized by the state as **Українська мова як іноземна (УМІ)**, mapped one-to-one onto CEFR and administered through the Ministry of Education's certification system:

| Guide level | CEFR / УМІ | Can-do target |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | A1-A2 | Cyrillic fluency; gender; nominative, accusative, and the **vocative**; present and gendered past |
| Intermediate | B1-B2 | Full seven-case system; aspect pairs; verbs of motion; case government |
| Advanced | C1-C2 | Professional and academic register; adverbial participles; stylistic range; surzhyk awareness |

B1 is the level required for Ukrainian citizenship — a useful anchor if the user has a concrete goal.

## Grammar Syllabus

The standard УМІ sequence. Within each level, earlier items are foundational and should be taught before later ones. This drives both the Grammar Corrections priority in Mode 1 and the Concept Spotlight in Mode 2.

- **Beginner (A1-A2)**: Cyrillic including і, ї, є, ґ → gender → **no articles, and є usually dropped in the present** → nominative → accusative → **the vocative case in everyday address** → present tense (both conjugations) → **past tense agreeing by gender** → locative after в/на → genitive
- **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: the full seven cases → **aspect pairs** → verbs of motion (іти/ходити, їхати/їздити) → dative and instrumental → case government of verbs and prepositions → numerals and their case demands → imperative → conditional with би/б
- **Advanced (C1-C2)**: adverbial participles (дієприслівники) → participial constructions — noting that **Ukrainian avoids the active participles Russian favors**, preferring subordinate clauses, which is itself a marker of good style → word order as information structure → register, and distinguishing standard Ukrainian from surzhyk

| Aspect | Beginner (A1-A2) | Intermediate (B1-B2) | Advanced (C1-C2) |

## Irregulars to Prioritize

Feeds the Irregularity Watch in `_common.md`. Ukrainian's signature alternation is one Russian does not have, and naming it early explains a great deal of what otherwise looks random.

**Class irregular** — teach the class, not the word:
- **The о/е → і alternation in closed syllables** — the most distinctively Ukrainian sound rule. A vowel becomes **і** when its syllable closes, and reverts when an ending reopens it: кінь → коня, ніч → ночі, стіл → стола, ніс → носа, Київ → Києва. Learners meeting *кінь* and *коня* assume two different words; name the alternation and a whole class of nouns becomes predictable.
- **Consonant alternations** before certain endings, fully systematic: к→ц→ч (рука → руці → ручка), г→з→ж (нога → нозі), х→с→ш (вухо → вусі). Fires in the dative and locative singular especially.
- **Fleeting vowels**: disappear when an ending is added — день → дня, вітер → вітру, орел → орла
- **Vocative formation** is regular and worth teaching as a rule rather than word by word: masculine -е/-у (Олексій → Олексію, друг → друже), feminine -о/-е (Ірина → Ірино, мама → мамо), and it is **obligatory** in natural Ukrainian.
- **Aspect pairs by prefix or suffix**: писати → написати, читати → прочитати, вирішувати → вирішити. Name which mechanism the pair uses.

**Locally irregular** — name where it's safe:
- Alternations fire only **before specific endings** — the dative and locative singular, and the vocative. Elsewhere the stem is stable; tell the learner which slots to watch rather than treating the noun as unstable throughout.
- The **past tense agrees by gender and number, not person** — four forms rather than six, and simpler than the present. Genuinely encouraging for beginners.

**Fully irregular** — must be memorized:
- **Suppletive aspect pairs**: брати → взяти, говорити → сказати, ловити → піймати, класти → покласти. High frequency, no shared root.
- **Mobile stress** — unwritten and shifting within a paradigm, as in Russian. Mark it on every new vocabulary item and flag any shift.
- **Irregular verbs**: бути, їсти, дати, знати, хотіти, могти, іти (past: йшов, not the expected form)
- **Genitive plural** — the least predictable form; give it outright rather than expecting derivation
- **Irregular plurals and stem changes**: людина → люди, дитина → діти, брат → брати, ім'я → імена, око → очі, вухо → вуха
- **Numerals**: 2-4 take the nominative plural, 5+ take the genitive plural — name the rule every time, since it looks arbitrary otherwise

**By level**: at A1-A2 prioritize бути, the vocative, and the о/е → і alternation; at B1-B2 the consonant alternations and suppletive aspect pairs; at C1-C2 genitive plural and participial stems.

## Error Categories to Watch For

In Mode 1 Grammar Corrections, prioritize: the 7-case system **including the vocative** (actively used in modern Ukrainian, unlike Russian where it survives only in fossils) — Олексію!, мамо!, Ірино!; verbal aspect pairs (писати/написати); gender agreement in the past tense and adjectives; freer word order, which can make direct English-to-Ukrainian order sound stilted rather than wrong; and absence of articles.

In Vocabulary Translations, flag Ukrainian/Russian false friends the learner may confuse: Ukrainian **неділя** "Sunday" vs. Russian неделя "week"; **червоний** "red" vs. Russian красный; **лихий** "wicked/fierce" vs. Russian лихой "dashing"; **пильно** "attentively" vs. Russian пыльно "dusty".

## Additional Deep-Dive Types

These join the rotation alongside Concept Spotlight and Word Family from `_common.md`.

- **Conjugation Table**: Use the table format and level scaling defined in `_common.md`, with these Ukrainian specifics:
  - **Persons**: full 6 (я / ти / він-вона-воно / ми / ви / вони) for present and future. **Mark the stress on every form.**
  - **The past tense breaks the person pattern** — use four rows (він / вона / воно / вони), since Ukrainian past agrees by gender and number rather than person. Show this shape explicitly.
  - **Intermediate**: the **aspect pair side by side** — imperfective present / imperfective past / perfective past / perfective future. This makes visible why the perfective has no present tense.
  - **Advanced**: adverbial participle forms derived from the same verb
  - **Pattern line**: name the conjugation, any stem alternation, and whether stress is fixed or mobile

- **Case Declension**: Pick one word from the Key Vocabulary and show 3 of its 7 case forms (nominative + two most relevant to the sentence), with a one-line example of when each is used. **Favor including the vocative** when the word is a name or a person — it is the case that most distinguishes Ukrainian from Russian.
  - Example — **книга** (knyha, "book"): Nominative *книга* — the book (subject); Genitive *книги* — of the book / no book; Instrumental *книгою* — with/by the book

- **Aspect Pair**: Pick one verb from the translation and show its perfective/imperfective partner (читати "to read, ongoing" vs. прочитати "to read to completion"), with a one-line example of each, and say whether the pair is prefixed, suffixed, or suppletive.

## Watch-outs

- **The vocative case**: genuinely distinctive to Ukrainian (Russian dropped it) — Ірина → Ірино!, Олексій → Олексію!, мама → мамо!, друг → друже! — flag every missed vocative; it is a frequent, visible marker of English (or Russian) influence
- **Aspect choice**: name why when correcting — one-time/completed → perfective (написати); ongoing/repeated → imperfective (писати)
- **Case after numbers and quantities**: 2-4 take the nominative plural, 5+ the genitive plural — flag with the rule
- **Ukrainian/Russian false friends**: learners with prior Russian exposure import similar-looking words with different senses — call these out explicitly
- **Overusing pronouns and "to be"**: *я є інженер* sounds unnatural; the natural zero-copula version is *я інженер*
- **The о/е → і alternation**: when a vocabulary word shows і in its base form, give the alternating stem too (кінь / коня) — otherwise the learner will not recognize the declined form as the same word
- **Russian calques in word choice**: a grammatically correct sentence can still read as surzhyk; when a more idiomatic native Ukrainian word exists, offer it as naturalness feedback rather than as an error
