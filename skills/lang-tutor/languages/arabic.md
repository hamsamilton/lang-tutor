# Arabic Tutor Guide

Read this together with `_common.md`, which defines the feedback block formats, the universal deep-dive types, and the baseline level tables. This file supplies what is specific to Arabic.

## Conventions

Default to Modern Standard Arabic (MSA); note major dialect differences (Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf) when relevant or when the user prefers a dialect. Always include transliteration at beginner and intermediate levels. Remember Arabic reads right-to-left.

## Framework Alignment: a diglossia caveat first

Arabic has **no single dominant proficiency framework**, and the reason matters pedagogically. MSA is the written and formal standard but nobody's mother tongue; the spoken dialects differ enough to be mutually difficult. A learner fluent in Levantine may score poorly on an MSA exam and vice versa, so state which variety a level refers to.

The frameworks actually in use:
- **ACTFL** (Novice / Intermediate / Advanced / Superior) — dominant in North American university programs
- **ALPT** (Arabic Language Proficiency Test) and the **ILR** scale for government work
- **CEFR**, adapted by European institutions and increasingly by Arabic-teaching bodies

| Guide level | ≈ ACTFL | ≈ CEFR | Can-do target (MSA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Novice–Intermediate Low | A1-A2 | Script fluency; ال and sun/moon letters; equational sentences; past tense; iḍāfa |
| Intermediate | Intermediate Mid–Advanced Low | B1-B2 | Present tense and moods; Forms I-X; broken plurals; dual; negation particles |
| Advanced | Advanced Mid–Superior | C1-C2 | Full iʿrāb case endings; passive; ḥāl and tamyīz; rhetorical and classical register |

## Grammar Syllabus

The standard MSA teaching sequence. Within each level, earlier items are foundational and should be taught before later ones. This drives both the Grammar Corrections priority in Mode 1 and the Concept Spotlight in Mode 2.

- **Beginner (A1-A2)**: alphabet and connected forms → sun and moon letters → the definite article ال → gender → **equational (verbless) sentences** → attached pronouns → **iḍāfa** (construct possession) → past tense (الماضي) with its suffix set → basic negation with ما and ليس
- **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: present tense (المضارع) with its prefix-suffix circumfix → **verb Forms I-X** and the meaning each pattern contributes → **broken plurals** → the dual → the four negations (لا، ما، لم، لن) and the moods they govern → subjunctive (المنصوب) and jussive (المجزوم) → كان and its sisters → إنّ and its sisters
- **Advanced (C1-C2)**: full case endings (الإعراب) in reading and production → the internal passive (المبني للمجهول) → ḥāl (circumstantial) and tamyīz (specification) constructions → verbal nouns (المصدر) in nominal style → rhetorical word order and classical register

Add this row to both level tables in `_common.md`:

| Aspect | Beginner (A1-A2) | Intermediate (B1-B2) | Advanced (C1-C2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar focus | script, ال and sun/moon letters, gender, equational sentences, attached pronouns, iḍāfa, past tense | present tense, Forms I-X, broken plurals, dual, four negations, subjunctive & jussive, كان and إنّ families | full iʿrāb, internal passive, ḥāl and tamyīz, maṣdar nominal style, rhetorical register |

## Irregulars to Prioritize

Feeds the Irregularity Watch in `_common.md`. Arabic's irregularity is almost entirely **root-shape** irregularity — a root containing و, ي, or ء behaves differently, and once the learner knows which category a root falls into, the behavior is predictable.

**Class irregular** — teach the class, not the word. The weak-root categories are the single most valuable thing to name:

| Category | Shape | Example | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **مثال** assimilated | و as 1st radical | وصل → يَصِل | the و drops in the present |
| **أجوف** hollow | و/ي as 2nd radical | قال → يقول · باع → يبيع | the middle vowel lengthens or drops by form |
| **ناقص** defective | و/ي as 3rd radical | دعا → يدعو · رمى → يرمي | the final vowel shifts and often disappears |
| **مضاعف** doubled | 2nd and 3rd radicals identical | مدّ → يمدّ | the two collapse into a shadda |
| **مهموز** hamzated | ء in any radical | أكل → يأكل | hamza seat changes by surrounding vowel |

- **Verb Forms I-X** are not irregularity but patterned derivation — Form II intensifies or makes causative, Form V is its reflexive, Form VII is passive/mediopassive, Form VIII reflexive, Form X requestive. Name the form whenever a derived verb appears; it turns vocabulary into arithmetic.
- **Sound plurals are the regular case**: masculine -ūn/-īn, feminine -āt. Say when a plural *is* regular, so the broken ones stand out.

**Locally irregular** — name where it's safe:
- Weak roots misbehave only in the slots where the weak letter meets a vowel; the personal prefixes and suffixes never change. The endings are among the most regular in any language — say so.
- Form I is the irregular one. **Forms II-X are highly predictable** even for weak roots, so a learner who finds Form I chaotic can be reassured that the derived forms are systematic.

**Fully irregular** — must be memorized:
- **Broken plurals** (جمع التكسير) — the defining Arabic irregularity. The plural restructures the word internally rather than adding an ending: كِتاب kitāb → كُتُب kutub, وَلَد walad → أولاد awlād, رَجُل rajul → رِجال rijāl. There are roughly thirty patterns, and **membership is per-word** — always give the plural with the singular, exactly as German requires.
- **The Form I imperfect vowel** is unpredictable: يَفْعَل / يَفْعِل / يَفْعُل must be learned per verb. Include it in the vocabulary entry.
- **ليس** — the negative copula, defective (it has no past or present in the usual sense) and irregular throughout
- **Number-gender polarity**: numbers 3-10 take the **opposite** gender to what they count — ثلاثة كتب (feminine numeral, masculine noun). Counterintuitive to the point of disbelief; name the rule rather than the form.
- **Non-human plural agreement**: any non-human plural takes **feminine singular** adjectives and verbs — كتب جديدة, not جديدون. Structurally irregular from an English viewpoint and worth flagging every time.
- The **"five nouns"** (أب، أخ، حم، فو، ذو) decline with long vowels rather than case endings

**By level**: at Novice/A1-A2 prioritize the plural of every new noun and the hollow verbs قال/كان; at Intermediate/B1-B2 the weak-root categories and Forms I-X; at Advanced/C1-C2 case endings on irregular nouns and the five nouns.

## Error Categories to Watch For

In Mode 1 Grammar Corrections, prioritize: gender agreement including the non-human plural rule, iḍāfa structure (no definite article on the first noun), verb-subject agreement and order, dual forms, wrong definiteness with ال, and broken plural formation.

In Vocabulary Translations, always give the plural alongside the singular.

## Additional Deep-Dive Types

These join the rotation alongside Concept Spotlight and Word Family from `_common.md`.

- **Conjugation Table**: Use the table format and level scaling defined in `_common.md`, with these Arabic specifics:
  - **Persons**: Arabic distinguishes gender in the 2nd and 3rd person and has a **dual**, giving up to thirteen forms. At beginner level trim to the practical six (أنا / أنتَ / أنتِ / هو / هي / نحن) and add the plurals and dual later — a full thirteen-row grid at A1 teaches nothing.
  - **Beginner**: past tense (الماضي) for one verb, then present (المضارع)
  - **Intermediate**: past / present / subjunctive / jussive side by side — this shows that the moods differ only in the final vowel, which is far less frightening than it sounds
  - **Advanced**: the same root across Forms I, II, and X, showing how the pattern shifts the meaning
  - **Pattern line**: name the root category (sound, assimilated, hollow, defective, doubled, hamzated) and the Form number, plus the imperfect vowel for Form I

- **Root & Pattern**: Pick one word from the Key Vocabulary, extract its (usually three-letter) root, and show 2-3 other common words built on the same root. Maximum 3 rows:

  | Word | Transliteration | Meaning | What to notice |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | [root letters] | ... | [core meaning] | The root — the consonant skeleton carrying the idea |
  | [derived word] | ... | ... | root + [pattern] → how the pattern shapes the meaning |
  | [derived word] | ... | ... | root + [pattern] → how the pattern shapes the meaning |

  (e.g. ك-ت-ب "writing": كتاب book, مكتب office/desk, مكتبة library)

- **Plural Patterns**: Pick one noun from the Key Vocabulary and show its plural, noting whether it's sound (-ūn/-āt) or broken (internal change), with one more example of the same pattern.

## Watch-outs

- **Non-human plural rule**: non-human plurals take feminine singular adjectives and verbs (كتب جديدة) — the most counterintuitive rule for English speakers; correct every miss with the rule
- **Iḍāfa**: "the book of the student" is كتاب الطالب — no ال on the first noun; flag English-style possession
- **Sun letters**: الشمس is pronounced ash-shams, not al-shams — note as pronunciation, never as a spelling error
- **MSA vs. dialect**: if the user mixes dialect words into MSA, identify them as dialect rather than wrong, and give the MSA equivalent
- **Hamza and final-yāʾ spelling**: treat hamza-seat and ى/ي variations gently — note the standard form without flagging it as a real error
- **Broken plurals**: never introduce a noun without its plural — there is no derivable rule, and the learner will otherwise form a sound plural by default
- **Number-gender polarity**: correct with the full rule, since the learner will hit the same wall on the next number
