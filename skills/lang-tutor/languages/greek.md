# Greek Tutor Guide

Read this together with `_common.md`, which defines the feedback block formats, the universal deep-dive types, and the baseline level tables. This file supplies what is specific to Greek.

## Conventions

Default to Modern (Demotic) Greek. Greek uses its own alphabet (Ελληνικό αλφάβητο) — include transliteration/romanization alongside Greek script at beginner and intermediate levels. Always give nouns with their article (ο/η/το).

## Framework Alignment: CEFR / Πιστοποίηση Ελληνομάθειας

Greek is certified by the Centre for the Greek Language through the Certificate of Attainment in Greek (Πιστοποίηση Ελληνομάθειας), mapped one-to-one onto CEFR:

| Guide level | CEFR | Can-do target |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | A1-A2 | Alphabet fluency; self, family, routine; nominative and accusative; present and simple past |
| Intermediate | B1-B2 | Study and work situations; genitive; the θα/να particle system; **aspect stems** under control |
| Advanced | C1-C2 | Professional and academic register; participles, passive and deponent verbs, καθαρεύουσα residue |

B1 is the level required for Greek citizenship — a useful anchor if the user has a concrete goal.

## Grammar Syllabus

The standard CEFR-aligned sequence. Within each level, earlier items are foundational and should be taught before later ones. This drives both the Grammar Corrections priority in Mode 1 and the Concept Spotlight in Mode 2.

- **Beginner (A1-A2)**: alphabet and pronunciation → three genders with articles ο/η/το → present tense (-ω and -άω/-ώ patterns) → nominative vs. accusative → plural forms → σε/από + accusative and article fusion (σε + το → στο) → introduction to θα for future → aorist for simple past
- **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: genitive for possession → **the two verb stems: imperfective and perfective**, and how θα/να select between them → weak object pronoun placement (proclitic before finite verbs, enclitic on imperatives) → the subjunctive with να → passive voice and deponent verbs → imperfect vs. aorist → comparatives → relative clauses (που, ο οποίος)
- **Advanced (C1-C2)**: participles (μετοχές) and their register → perfect tenses with έχω → conditional structures → **καθαρεύουσα residue** in fixed and formal expressions (ancient endings surviving in bureaucratic and academic Greek) → stylistic and idiomatic range

## Irregulars to Prioritize

Feeds the Irregularity Watch in `_common.md`. Greek's central irregularity is the **aorist stem** — a verb's past form is often unguessable from its present, and this is where learners stall.

**Class irregular** — teach the class, not the word:
- **Aorist stem formation by consonant fusion**, entirely predictable once the rule is named: labials (π, β, φ, πτ) + σ → **ψ** (γράφω → έγραψα); velars (κ, γ, χ, χν) + σ → **ξ** (τρέχω → έτρεξα, ανοίγω → άνοιξα); dentals (τ, δ, θ, ζ) + σ → **σ** (πείθω → έπεισα). Give the rule, not the individual form.
- **Contracted verbs** (-άω/-ώ) form a second conjugation with their own endings: αγαπάω/αγαπώ, μπορώ, ζω. Two sub-patterns (A: αγαπώ; B: μπορώ) — name which one applies.
- **Deponent verbs** (media tantum) carry passive endings with active meaning: έρχομαι, κάθομαι, σκέφτομαι, εργάζομαι. Learners try to "de-passivize" them; flag membership.
- **Neuter -μα nouns extend their stem** in the genitive: το πρόβλημα → του προβλήματος, το όνομα → του ονόματος. A large, fully predictable class.

**Locally irregular** — name where it's safe:
- Most Greek verbs are irregular **only in the aorist stem**. Once that stem is known, the endings are perfectly regular, and the imperfective (present, imperfect, future continuous) is regular throughout. Say this — it halves the apparent problem.
- **Stress placement** shifts under the three-syllable rule: ο άνθρωπος → του ανθρώπου, το μάθημα → τα μαθήματα. This is regular (the accent may not fall further back than the third syllable from the end) but feels arbitrary until the rule is named.

**Fully irregular** — must be memorized:
- **Suppletive aorists** — the highest-frequency verbs share no root with their past forms: βλέπω → είδα, λέω → είπα, τρώω → έφαγα, πηγαίνω → πήγα, παίρνω → πήρα, έρχομαι → ήρθα, δίνω → έδωσα. These are the most common verbs in the language, so they cannot be deferred.
- **είμαι** (to be) — irregular throughout, and the only Greek verb with no aorist in the usual sense
- **Ancient-derived plurals and forms**: το κρέας → τα κρέατα, το φως → τα φώτα, το γάλα → τα γάλατα
- **Irregular comparatives**: καλός → καλύτερος, κακός → χειρότερος, πολύς → περισσότερος, μεγάλος → μεγαλύτερος

**By level**: at A1-A2 prioritize είμαι, έχω, and the suppletive aorists of the top ten verbs; at B1-B2 the consonant-fusion rules and deponent verbs; at C1-C2 καθαρεύουσα forms and participial stems.

## Error Categories to Watch For

In Mode 1 Grammar Corrections, prioritize: the 4-case system (nominative/genitive/accusative/vocative) reshaping article, noun, and adjective; strict gender-case-number agreement across ο/η/το and adjectives; **verb aspect** — perfective vs. imperfective stems, especially after θα/να; weak object pronoun placement (*το βλέπω* vs. *δες το*); prepositions σε and από governing the accusative with article fusion (σε + το → στο).

Flag overly formal (καθαρεύουσα-flavored) phrasing when casual Demotic register clearly fits, and vice versa.

In Vocabulary Translations, flag false friends proactively: *συμπαθητικός* (likeable, not sympathetic), *ευτυχώς* (fortunately), *χαρακτήρας* (personality/character), *εντάξει* (okay/fine), *εμπάθεια* (malice, **not** empathy).

## Additional Deep-Dive Types

These join the rotation alongside Concept Spotlight and Word Family from `_common.md`.

- **Conjugation Table**: Use the table format and level scaling defined in `_common.md`, with these Greek specifics:
  - **Persons**: full 6 (εγώ / εσύ / αυτός-ή-ό / εμείς / εσείς / αυτοί-ές-ά). Subject pronouns are normally dropped, so present the forms without them past A1. **Mark the accent on every form** — it shifts, and an unaccented table teaches the wrong pronunciation.
  - **Beginner (A1-A2)**: present tense, then the aorist — showing the stem change explicitly
  - **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: the **two stems side by side** — imperfective (present, imperfect, θα + present) against perfective (aorist, θα + perfective, να + perfective). This is the single most useful Greek table, because it shows that aspect, not tense, drives the choice.
  - **Advanced (C1-C2)**: active vs. passive/deponent paradigms, or perfect tenses with έχω
  - **Pattern line**: name the conjugation (-ω, -άω/-ώ type A or B, deponent) and how the aorist stem is formed

- **Case Declension**: Pick one noun from the translation (or Key Vocabulary) and show it across 2-3 cases in a small table:

  | Case | Greek | Transliteration | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Nominative | ο άνθρωπος | o ánthropos | the person (subject) |
  | Genitive | του ανθρώπου | tou anthrópou | of the person (possession) |
  | Accusative | τον άνθρωπο | ton ánthropo | the person (object) |

  Note what changes and why: the article changes with the noun, the ending shifts (often -ος → -ου → -ο), **and the stress may move** — flag the accent shift, it is a common pronunciation trap.

- **Word Family**: Pick one Greek root from the Key Vocabulary and show 2-3 related Greek words alongside the English loanwords they gave rise to (e.g. *φιλοσοφία → philosophy*, *φίλος* "friend" → *-phile*, *σοφία* "wisdom" → *sophomore*). Note how recognizing the root makes both the Greek word and its English relative easier to remember.

## Watch-outs

- **Case endings**: nominative/genitive/accusative each reshape the article AND the noun ending — name the case on every correction, not just "wrong ending"
- **Gender agreement**: article, noun, and adjective must all agree in gender, case, and number — flag every mismatch
- **Verb aspect over tense**: θα/να pair with the perfective stem for a single completed action (θα γράψω) vs. the imperfective for ongoing/habitual (θα γράφω) — English speakers default to tense and miss this entirely
- **Weak pronoun placement**: object pronouns go BEFORE the finite verb (τον βλέπω) but AFTER imperatives and gerunds (δες τον) — a frequent word-order slip
- **False friends**: *συμπαθητικός*, *ευτυχώς*, *χαρακτήρας*, *εντάξει*, *εμπάθεια* — call out proactively
- **Accent placement is meaning-bearing**: πότε (when?) / ποτέ (never), νόμος (law) / νομός (province) — flag whenever a minimal pair appears in vocabulary
- **Final -ν rules**: τον/την keep the -ν before certain consonants and drop it before others — a small but visible marker of fluency worth correcting at intermediate level
