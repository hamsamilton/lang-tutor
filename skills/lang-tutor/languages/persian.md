# Persian (Farsi) Tutor Guide

Read this together with `_common.md`, which defines the feedback block formats, the universal deep-dive types, and the baseline level tables. This file supplies what is specific to Persian.

## Conventions

Default to Iranian Farsi (note Dari and Tajik as related varieties only briefly, when relevant). Persian uses the Perso-Arabic script and reads right-to-left. Always include transliteration at beginner and intermediate levels.

## Framework Alignment: AZFA / CEFR

Persian teaching is organized around **AZFA** (آموزش زبان فارسی به غیرفارسی‌زبانان), the standard curriculum used by the Dehkhoda Institute and the Saadi Foundation, increasingly mapped onto CEFR:

| Guide level | ≈ CEFR | Can-do target |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | A1-A2 | Script; SOV order; present and past stems; ezafe; را; everyday conversation |
| Intermediate | B1-B2 | Subjunctive; compound verbs in depth; perfect tenses; **colloquial vs. written register** |
| Advanced | C1-C2 | Literary and classical register; ezafe chains; poetry and formal prose; full stylistic range |

**A structural note worth telling learners early**: Persian has an unusually wide split between written Persian (نوشتاری) and spoken Persian (محاوره‌ای) — not merely slang, but systematic sound changes affecting the most common words. A learner who studies only the written form will not follow a conversation. Name which register a translation uses.

## Grammar Syllabus

The standard AZFA sequence. Within each level, earlier items are foundational and should be taught before later ones. This drives both the Grammar Corrections priority in Mode 1 and the Concept Spotlight in Mode 2.

- **Beginner (A1-A2)**: Perso-Arabic script → **no grammatical gender and no articles** → SOV word order → **the present-stem / past-stem duality** → present indicative with می- → simple past → personal endings (so pronouns are usually dropped) → **ezafe** → **را** as the definite object marker → possessive suffixes → introduction to compound verbs
- **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: subjunctive (بروم) and the many structures requiring it → future → present perfect and past perfect → **compound verbs in depth** (the productive core of the Persian lexicon) → passive with شدن → conditionals → relative clauses with که → comparative and superlative → **the colloquial sound changes** as a system
- **Advanced (C1-C2)**: literary vs. colloquial register in full → long ezafe chains → classical and poetic word order → Arabic-derived vocabulary and its plurals → nominal style and formal correspondence

Add this row to both level tables in `_common.md`:

| Aspect | Beginner (A1-A2) | Intermediate (B1-B2) | Advanced (C1-C2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar focus (AZFA) | script, no gender, SOV, present/past stems, می-, past tense, personal endings, ezafe, را | subjunctive, perfect tenses, compound verbs, passive with شدن, conditionals, که clauses, colloquial forms | literary register, ezafe chains, classical word order, Arabic plurals, nominal style |

## Irregulars to Prioritize

Feeds the Irregularity Watch in `_common.md`. Persian verb **endings** are perfectly regular — every verb takes the same personal suffixes. The entire irregularity of the language sits in one place: the present stem.

**The central irregularity** — the **present stem cannot be derived from the infinitive** and must be learned with it, exactly like an English irregular verb:

| Infinitive | Meaning | Present stem | Present form |
|---|---|---|---|
| رفتن *raftan* | to go | رو *rav-* | می‌روم *miravam* |
| دیدن *didan* | to see | بین *bin-* | می‌بینم *mibinam* |
| گفتن *goftan* | to say | گو *gu-* | می‌گویم *miguyam* |
| کردن *kardan* | to do | کن *kon-* | می‌کنم *mikonam* |
| توانستن *tavānestan* | to be able | توان *tavān-* | می‌توانم *mitavānam* |
| نشستن *neshastan* | to sit | نشین *neshin-* | می‌نشینم *mineshinam* |

Always give the present stem alongside a new verb — a verb learned without it cannot be conjugated at all.

**Class irregular** — teach the class, not the word:
- **The colloquial sound changes are systematic**, not random slang. Long ā becomes u before a nasal (نان *nān* → نون *nun*, خانه *khāne* → خونه *khune*); است reduces to -ه; را becomes رو; می‌روم becomes می‌رم *miram*. Name the rule and the learner can convert any word themselves.
- **Compound verbs** are the productive engine of Persian: a noun or adjective plus a light verb (کردن، شدن، دادن، زدن، خوردن). Only the light verb conjugates — صحبت می‌کنم, never *صحبت‌کردن‌ام. Name the light verb and its contribution.
- **Plurals**: **-ها is universal and always safe.** -ان is used for animates in more formal register. Say this — it means the learner can always form a correct plural.

**Locally irregular** — name where it's safe:
- Once the present stem is known, **everything else is regular**: the personal endings, the negative prefix ن-, the subjunctive prefix ب-, the imperative. The irregularity is confined to a single morpheme.
- The **past stem is always the infinitive minus -ن** — never irregular. So half the verb system is fully predictable.

**Fully irregular** — must be memorized:
- **بودن** (to be) and its enclitic forms (-ام، -ی، -است…), plus داشتن, which does not take می- in the present (دارم, not *می‌دارم)
- **Arabic broken plurals** in borrowed vocabulary: کتاب → کتب, عالم → علما, حق → حقوق. These coexist with the regular -ها form, and the broken plural usually carries a more formal register — note which is appropriate rather than marking either wrong.
- **Arabic-derived feminine -ه words** and irregular Arabic-pattern nouns that resist Persian regularization

**By level**: at A1-A2 prioritize the present stems of the top twenty verbs and بودن/داشتن; at B1-B2 the compound-verb families and the colloquial conversions; at C1-C2 Arabic plurals and register-paired synonyms.

## Error Categories to Watch For

In Mode 1 Grammar Corrections, prioritize: SOV word order (the verb goes last), the ezafe linker connecting a noun to its modifier or possessor (never written in the script), compound verb integrity (the noun and light verb must stay paired correctly), dropped pronouns where the personal ending already encodes the subject, and **را** marking definite direct objects.

## Additional Deep-Dive Types

These join the rotation alongside Concept Spotlight and Word Family from `_common.md`.

- **Conjugation Table**: Use the table format and level scaling defined in `_common.md`, with these Persian specifics:
  - **Persons**: full 6 (من / تو / او / ما / شما / آنها). Subject pronouns are normally dropped since the ending carries the person — present the forms without them past A1.
  - **Always head the table with both stems** — infinitive, past stem, and present stem — since the present stem is the unguessable part
  - **Beginner (A1-A2)**: present indicative with می-, then simple past
  - **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: present / past / present perfect / **subjunctive** side by side; optionally add a colloquial column showing the spoken contraction of each form, which is the highest-value addition for a learner who wants to converse
  - **Advanced (C1-C2)**: the full mood range including past subjunctive and conditional
  - **Pattern line**: give the present stem explicitly and note whether the verb is simple or compound

- **Ezafe Chain**: Pick one short possessive or descriptive phrase from the translation and break it into its individual ezafe links, showing how each -e/-ye connects the words even though it isn't written. Maximum 3-4 links:

  | Word | Ezafe (unwritten) | Transliteration | What it links |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | [noun] | -e/-ye | ... | head noun |
  | [modifier/possessor] | -e/-ye (if chain continues) | ... | links to next word |
  | [final word] | — | ... | end of chain |

  (e.g. کتابِ دوستِ من → *ketāb-e dust-e man*, "the book of my friend")

- **Compound Verb Family**: Pick one light verb (کردن or شدن) and show it combined with 2-3 different nouns, noting how the meaning shifts with each pairing. Maximum 3 rows:

  | Compound Verb | Transliteration | Meaning | Note |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | [noun] + کردن/شدن | ... | ... | how the noun shapes the resulting meaning |

  (e.g. صحبت کردن "to talk," کار کردن "to work," خسته شدن "to become tired")

## Watch-outs

- **Verb-final order**: the verb must come last — من به مدرسه می‌روم, not English-order; correct any SVO carryover
- **Unwritten ezafe**: کتاب من is pronounced *ketāb-e man*, but the -e is never written — don't let the user "spell" it, only flag it in transliteration
- **را with definite objects**: کتاب را خواندم ("I read the book") vs. کتاب خواندم ("I read a book") — dropping را or adding it to an indefinite object is among the most common learner errors
- **Compound verbs as a unit**: only کردن conjugates (صحبت می‌کنم) — the noun stays fixed while the light verb inflects
- **No gender, but don't over-relax**: او covers "he/she" alike and adjectives never agree — flag if the user imports gendered agreement from another language
- **Present stems**: never introduce a verb without its present stem — the learner cannot conjugate it otherwise, and the stem is unguessable
- **Register mixing**: colloquial contractions in formal writing (or stiff written forms in casual chat) read as oddly as a grammar error — name which register the sentence is aiming at
