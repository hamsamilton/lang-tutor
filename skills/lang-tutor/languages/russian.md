# Russian Tutor Guide

Read this together with `_common.md`, which defines the feedback block formats, the universal deep-dive types, and the baseline level tables. This file supplies what is specific to Russian.

## Conventions

At beginner level, include transliteration for all Russian text and **mark word stress** on new vocabulary (e.g. молокó) — stress placement isn't written but changes pronunciation and sometimes meaning.

## Framework Alignment: ТРКИ / TORFL

Russian proficiency is certified by ТРКИ (Тест по русскому языку как иностранному), which maps onto CEFR:

| Guide level | ТРКИ / TORFL | CEFR | Can-do target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | ТЭУ (Элементарный), ТБУ (Базовый) | A1-A2 | ~780-1300 words; survival conversation, self and routine, three or four cases in practice |
| Intermediate | ТРКИ-1, ТРКИ-2 | B1-B2 | ~2300-6000 words; study and work situations, full case system, aspect under control |
| Advanced | ТРКИ-3, ТРКИ-4 | C1-C2 | Professional and academic register; participles, gerunds, stylistic range |

ТРКИ-1 is the level required for admission to Russian universities, and ТРКИ-2 for most professional qualification — useful anchors if the user has a concrete goal.

## Grammar Syllabus

The standard ТРКИ sequence. Within each level, earlier items are foundational and should be taught before later ones. This drives both the Grammar Corrections priority in Mode 1 and the Concept Spotlight in Mode 2.

- **Beginner (A1-A2)**: Cyrillic → gender → **no articles and no present-tense "to be"** → nominative → prepositional (location) → accusative (direct object) → present tense conjugation (1st and 2nd patterns) → **past tense agreeing by gender, not person** → plural → genitive (possession, absence, after numbers)
- **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: dative and instrumental → the full six-case system in interaction → **verbal aspect pairs** (imperfective vs. perfective) → verbs of motion, unprefixed (идти/ходить) then prefixed (пойти, прийти, уйти) → case government of common verbs and prepositions → imperative → conditional with бы → numerals and their case demands
- **Advanced (C1-C2)**: active and passive participles (причастия) → verbal adverbs (деепричастия) → word-order as information structure → impersonal constructions → colloquial vs. literary register → stylistic and idiomatic range

## Irregulars to Prioritize

Feeds the Irregularity Watch in `_common.md`. Russian's hardest irregularities are not verb endings — they are **stress** and **aspect pairing**, both of which are invisible in writing.

**Class irregular** — teach the class, not the word:
- **Consonant mutation in the 1sg**: 2nd-conjugation verbs mutate their final stem consonant in the *я* form only — писа́ть → пишу́, ви́деть → ви́жу, проси́ть → прошу́, е́здить → е́зжу. The mutations are systematic (с→ш, д→ж, т→ч, з→ж, ст→щ).
- **л-insertion** after labials in the 1sg: люби́ть → люблю́, купи́ть → куплю́, спать → сплю
- **Fleeting vowels**: a vowel in the final syllable disappears when an ending is added — оте́ц → отца́, день → дня, ку́сок → куска́. Predictable by pattern, invisible from the nominative.
- **Aspect pairs by prefix**: most pairs are formed with a prefix (де́лать → сде́лать, писа́ть → написа́ть) or a suffix alternation (реши́ть → реша́ть). Name which mechanism a given pair uses.

**Locally irregular** — name where it's safe:
- Verb irregularity in Russian is usually confined to the **1sg present** (mutation, л-insertion) or to the **past-tense stem**. The remaining forms follow the ending set predictably — say which slot misbehaves.
- The **past tense has only four forms** (masculine, feminine, neuter, plural) and agrees by gender rather than person, so it is far simpler than the present. Beginners find this genuinely encouraging.

**Fully irregular** — must be memorized:
- **Suppletive aspect pairs**: most pairs share a root, but the highest-frequency ones do not — говори́ть/сказа́ть, брать/взять, класть/положи́ть, лови́ть/пойма́ть. These must be learned as vocabulary pairs, not derived.
- **Mobile stress** — the deepest irregularity in the language, because stress is unwritten yet shifts within a paradigm: рука́ → ру́ку → ру́ки, окно́ → о́кна, нача́ть → на́чал → начала́. Stress can also distinguish words outright: за́мок (castle) / замо́к (lock), му́ка (torment) / мука́ (flour). Mark stress on every new vocabulary item and flag any shift.
- **Genitive plural** — the most irregular form in Russian; endings (-ов, -ей, -∅, plus inserted vowels) resist rules. Give the form explicitly rather than expecting derivation.
- **Irregular plurals**: друг → друзья́, брат → бра́тья, сын → сыновья́, ребёнок → де́ти, челове́к → лю́ди, год → лет (in the genitive plural after numbers)
- **Indeclinable loanwords**: ко́фе, метро́, пальто́, такси́, кино́ — never change form; learners decline them by analogy and should be told not to
- **Numerals**: 2-4 take genitive **singular**, 5+ take genitive **plural**, and 1 agrees like an adjective — arbitrary-looking and worth naming as a rule every time

**By level**: at A1-A2 prioritize stress marking, the zero copula, and gendered past forms; at B1-B2 aspect pairs (especially the suppletive ones) and 1sg mutations; at C1-C2 genitive plural, participial stems, and stress shifts in derived forms.

## Error Categories to Watch For

In Mode 1 Grammar Corrections, prioritize: case endings (especially after prepositions and numbers), verbal aspect choice (perfective vs. imperfective), verbs of motion (идти/ходить, ехать/ездить), gender agreement in past tense, unnecessary pronouns or articles carried over from English.

## Additional Deep-Dive Types

These join the rotation alongside Concept Spotlight and Word Family from `_common.md`.

- **Conjugation Table**: Use the table format and level scaling defined in `_common.md`, with these Russian specifics:
  - **Persons**: full 6 (я / ты / он-она-оно / мы / вы / они) for present and future. **Mark the stress on every form** — an unstressed table teaches the wrong word.
  - **The past tense breaks the person pattern**: use four rows (он / она / оно / они) instead, since Russian past agrees by gender and number rather than person. Show this shape explicitly — it surprises learners.
  - **Beginner (A1-A2)**: present tense, then the four-form past
  - **Intermediate (B1-B2)**: the **aspect pair side by side** — imperfective present / imperfective past / perfective past / perfective future. This is the single most useful Russian table, because it shows why perfective has no present tense.
  - **Advanced (C1-C2)**: participial and gerund forms derived from the same verb
  - **Pattern line**: name the conjugation (1st or 2nd), any 1sg mutation, and whether stress is fixed or mobile

- **Root Family**: Pick one word from the Key Vocabulary and show 2-3 relatives built on the same root with different prefixes/suffixes (e.g. ходить: вы́ход exit, прихо́д arrival, похо́д hike). Note how each prefix shifts the meaning.

- **Aspect Pair**: Pick one verb from the translation and show its perfective/imperfective partner, with a one-line example of when each is used. Say whether the pair is formed by prefix, suffix, or suppletion.

## Watch-outs

- **Aspect choice**: the deepest English-speaker struggle — when correcting, always name why (one-time completed → perfective; ongoing/repeated → imperfective)
- **Case after numbers**: 2-4 take genitive singular, 5+ take genitive plural — flag it with the rule, it looks arbitrary otherwise
- **Verbs of motion**: unidirectional vs. multidirectional (идти vs. ходить) — correct with the round-trip test
- **Mobile stress**: when a vocabulary word's stress moves in declension (рука́ → ру́ку) or distinguishes words (за́мок castle / замо́к lock), point it out
- **Overusing pronouns and "to be"**: я есть инженер is wrong — show the natural zero-copula version
- **Genitive plural**: give the form outright rather than letting the learner derive it — this is where confident intermediate students break
- **Animacy in the accusative**: masculine animate nouns take the genitive form in the accusative (ви́жу студе́нта, not студе́нт) — name the animacy rule when correcting
