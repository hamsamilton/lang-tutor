# Vietnamese Tutor Guide

Read this together with `_common.md`, which defines the feedback block formats, the universal deep-dive types, and the baseline level tables. This file supplies what is specific to Vietnamese.

## Conventions

Always show Vietnamese with full tone marks — tones are meaning-bearing (ma/má/mà/mả/mã/mạ are six different words). Per the main rules, don't flag the user's *missing* diacritics as errors, but always include the fully marked form in your feedback so they see it. Default to northern (Hanoi) standard; note southern differences when relevant.

## Framework Alignment: Khung năng lực tiếng Việt / CEFR

Vietnam's Ministry of Education issued a six-level framework for Vietnamese as a foreign language (**Khung năng lực tiếng Việt dùng cho người nước ngoài**, 2015), grouped into three bands and aligned to CEFR:

| Guide level | Vietnamese framework | ≈ CEFR | Can-do target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Sơ cấp — bậc 1-2 | A1-A2 | Six tones; SVO with noun-adjective order; classifiers; kinship pronouns; time words |
| Intermediate | Trung cấp — bậc 3-4 | B1-B2 | Aspect markers; được/bị passive; comparison; serial verbs; relative clauses |
| Advanced | Cao cấp — bậc 5-6 | C1-C2 | Sentence-final particles; Sino-Vietnamese register; regional variation; idiom |

## Grammar Syllabus

The standard sequence. Within each level, earlier items are foundational and should be taught before later ones. This drives both the Grammar Corrections priority in Mode 1 and the Concept Spotlight in Mode 2.

- **Beginner (bậc 1-2)**: the **six tones** and the alphabet's diacritics → SVO word order → **modifiers follow the noun** (nhà lớn) → **classifiers** (cái/con/chiếc/quyển) → **kinship pronouns** as the pronoun system → yes/no questions with …không? → the optional tense markers đã/đang/sẽ → possession with của → negation không/chưa
- **Intermediate (bậc 3-4)**: aspect and completion with rồi → **passive with được (favorable) vs. bị (unfavorable)** → comparison with hơn/nhất → serial verb constructions → result and direction complements → relative clauses with mà → question forms (đã…chưa?, có…không?) → conjunctions and discourse connectives
- **Advanced (bậc 5-6)**: **sentence-final particles** (nhé/đấy/cơ/mà/thôi) and the nuance each carries → the **Sino-Vietnamese vocabulary layer** and its register → northern vs. southern usage → formal, journalistic, and literary style → idiom and proverb

## Irregulars to Prioritize

Feeds the Irregularity Watch in `_common.md`. Vietnamese is **isolating** — words never change form, so there is no conjugation, declension, or agreement to be irregular. Say this early; it is a genuine and large simplification. The irregularity lives instead in three pairings that must be learned per word.

**Class irregular** — teach the class, not the word:
- **Sino-Vietnamese vs. native doublets**: most concepts have two words, one borrowed from Chinese and one native, differing in register and in what they can combine with — quốc / nước (country), thủy / nước (water), nhân / người (person), hỏa / lửa (fire). The Sino-Vietnamese form is bound and appears in compounds; the native form stands alone. Name which layer a word belongs to — it explains why *nước Mỹ* is fine but *quốc Mỹ* is not.
- **Reduplication with tone change** softens or intensifies: đỏ → đo đỏ (reddish), nhẹ → nhè nhẹ (rather light). Regular once named, baffling otherwise.
- **Kinship pronouns are a system, not a list**: the choice encodes relative age, gender, and social distance, and both speaker and listener terms shift together. Teach the axis, not the individual words.

**Locally irregular** — name where it's safe:
- **Tense markers are optional**, not required. đã/đang/sẽ are used only when the time is not already clear from context or a time word. Learners over-apply them out of English habit; telling them the markers are droppable is both correct and freeing.
- **Southern Vietnamese merges two tones** (hỏi and ngã are pronounced alike), so a southern speaker's spelling errors on those two are systematic rather than careless. Note the variety before correcting.

**Fully irregular** — must be memorized:
- **Classifier-noun pairings** are arbitrary and per-noun: cái for most inanimate objects, con for animals (and, unpredictably, con dao "knife" and con đường "road"), quyển/cuốn for books, chiếc for vehicles and one-of-a-pair items, tờ for sheets, bức for pictures and letters. Always give the classifier with a new noun — a noun learned without it cannot be counted.
- **One English word splitting into several Vietnamese ones**: "wear" is mặc (clothes) / đội (hats) / đeo (glasses, jewelry) / đi (shoes); "carry" splits similarly. The learner cannot infer the split — flag it when the wrong member appears.
- **Tone minimal pairs** in high-frequency vocabulary: ma / má / mà / mả / mã / mạ; mua (buy) / mưa (rain) / múa (dance); bàn (table) / bán (sell) / bạn (friend). Show the contrast explicitly when one appears.

**By level**: at bậc 1-2 prioritize the classifier of every new noun and tone minimal pairs; at bậc 3-4 the được/bị distinction and verb-splitting vocabulary; at bậc 5-6 the Sino-Vietnamese register pairs.

## Error Categories to Watch For

In Mode 1 Grammar Corrections, prioritize: missing or wrong classifiers (cái/con/chiếc/quyển), modifier order (adjectives follow nouns: nhà lớn, not lớn nhà), pronoun/kinship-term choice for the relationship, misuse of tense markers (đã/đang/sẽ — often optional), and word choice where one English word maps to several Vietnamese ones.

## Additional Deep-Dive Types

These join the rotation alongside Concept Spotlight and Word Family from `_common.md`.

> **Note on conjugation tables**: the conjugation table in `_common.md` does **not** apply — Vietnamese words never inflect. The deep-dives below take its place.

- **Kinship Pronouns**: Take the pronouns from the translation and show how they'd change for 2-3 different relationships (older man → anh, older woman → chị, younger person → em), with a one-line note on what choosing each one signals. Because there is no neutral "you," this is the highest-value Vietnamese deep-dive — favor it whenever the translation addresses someone.

- **Tone Contrast**: Pick one syllable from the Key Vocabulary and show the words its six tones produce. Omit rows where the tone forms no real word:

  | Vietnamese | Tone | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | ma | ngang (level) | ghost |
  | má | sắc (rising) | cheek / mother (southern) |
  | mà | huyền (falling) | but / that |
  | mả | hỏi (dipping) | grave |
  | mã | ngã (creaky rising) | horse (Sino-Viet) / code |
  | mạ | nặng (heavy) | rice seedling |

- **Compound Words**: Pick one syllable from the Key Vocabulary and show 2-3 common compounds it forms, noting the shared meaning thread (e.g. học "study": học sinh student, đại học university, học phí tuition). Where the syllable is Sino-Vietnamese, say so — it explains why it cannot stand alone.

## Watch-outs

- **Tone minimal pairs**: when a vocabulary word has a common tonal near-twin (mua to buy / mưa rain / múa to dance), show the contrast explicitly
- **Classifiers**: cái for most objects, con for animals (and some objects), quyển/cuốn for books — correct every bare-noun count at beginner level
- **Pronouns are relationships**: there's no neutral "you" — using the wrong kinship term reads as rude or odd, so treat pronoun choice as grammar, not style
- **Word order**: modifiers follow the noun, questions don't invert — flag English-order sentences
- **One English word, many Vietnamese words**: "wear" is mặc / đội / đeo — point out the split when the user picks the wrong one
- **được vs. bị**: both form the passive, but được frames the event as fortunate and bị as unfortunate — choosing the wrong one changes the speaker's attitude, not just the grammar
- **Over-marking tense**: if the sentence already has a time word, đã/sẽ is usually redundant — suggest dropping it as naturalness feedback rather than flagging it as an error
